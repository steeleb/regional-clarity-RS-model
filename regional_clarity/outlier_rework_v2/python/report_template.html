What changed vs. v1

Same pipeline, two additions, one new folder

Everything upstream of feature-group modeling is byte-for-byte identical to v1: the same 12,637 field–satellite matchups, the same four-stage outlier-filtering methodology (site geometry, metadata/cloud QA, reflectance-outlier RANSAC, heavy-rain gap filter), the same gridMET antecedent-weather summaries, and the same LakeCat catchment characteristics. Those outputs were copied into outlier_rework_v2/ rather than regenerated, so this folder works standalone even if outlier_rework/ is later removed - nothing here re-hits gridMET, LakeCat, or the elevation API.

Two things changed, both requested as follow-ups at the end of the v1 report:

  1. Shoreline-proximity flag added as a model feature. The siteSR pipeline already computes a per-site distance to its matched waterbody's shoreline, thresholded into a QA flag - it was sitting unused in a CSV v1's own site-characteristics script already read, just not selected. See Feature engineering, below, for the exact definition and encoding.
  2. Gap-aware hyperparameter selection extended to every tuning step. v1 only used gap-aware selection (pick the smallest train-val gap among trials within 2% of the best CV RMSE, instead of the single lowest-RMSE trial) at the very last tuning step. The single-model baseline, the feature-group ablation, and by extension the fixed hyperparameters backward elimination reuses were all still on the plain, gap-blind objective. v1 flagged this as the same failure mode its final-tuning fix was built to address, present at every earlier stage.

A third piece of technical debt got fixed along the way, not requested but necessary to add a feature safely: v1 hardcoded its selected 16-feature set as a copy-pasted Python literal in three separate files, with no code path connecting it back to backward elimination's actual output. v2's backward elimination now writes results/selected_features.json once, and every downstream script reads it - the kind of drift risk that matters most exactly when the feature set is changing.

Feature engineering

Site characteristics: 7 candidates → 8

The optical (17) and antecedent-weather (20) candidate feature groups are unchanged from v1. One feature was added to the site-characteristics group:

FeatureDefinitionSource
elevation_msite elevationUSGS 3DEP, via elevatr
catchment_area_sqkm, pct_impervious_2006, pct_urban_2006, pct_forest_2006, pct_cropland_2006, pct_wetland_2006catchment land cover, 2006 vintageLakeCat (StreamCatTools)
shoreline_flag (new)0 = open water, 1 = near-shore (≤230m optical-pixel buffer), 2 = site falls outside its matched waterbody polygonsiteSR pipeline's dist_to_shore/flag_optical_shoreline/flag_wb, already computed for QA but never previously consumed downstream

The underlying dist_to_shore value (continuous, meters) was collapsed to this 3-level integer category rather than passed through directly, so the ~4% of sites (62 of 1,525) that fall outside their matched waterbody polygon - and would otherwise be missing outright, since distance-to-shore is undefined for them - get an explicit third category instead of a gap. Across the 1,525 sites in the training population: 629 open water, 834 near-shore, 62 outside their waterbody. It is treated as an ordinary integer feature (fed to correlation pruning and both tree models and the neural net the same way atm_corr_LaSRC already is), not one-hot encoded - consistent with how the pipeline already handles its other binary/categorical inputs.

A rare feature that survives on its own merit in every model

shoreline_flag is not eliminated in any of the three independent per-model eliminations, nor in the joint elimination - it clears the shadow-permutation noise floor for XGBoost, LightGBM, and the neural net alike. Very few candidates in this pipeline have cleared that bar this consistently; most of what gets added to this feature set (see v1's antecedent-weather variables) survives for at most one or two architectures.

Gap-aware tuning & final evaluation

Gap-aware from the start: a smaller mean gap for two of three models, and a better final test result for two of three

Modelv1 mean gap (gap-aware only at final step)v2 mean gap (gap-aware throughout)Partition 3 gap (hardest fold)

Applying gap-aware selection at the baseline and feature-group stages too - not just at this final step - modestly shrinks the average train-val gap for XGBoost and, more sharply, for the neural net; it's essentially flat for LightGBM. Partition 3 remains the largest single-fold gap for every model regardless, exactly as v1 found - consistent with the outlier-HUC investigation's separate conclusion that partition 3's difficulty is a physical signal-compression property of its HUC4 1701 lake cluster, not a tuning-objective artifact. Gap-aware tuning was never expected to close that gap on its own; it was designed to stop the tuner from actively making the average gap worse, and on that narrower claim it holds up for two of the three architectures.

Both tree models improve on v1's final production result

XGBoost's test RMSE improves from 1.860m (v1) to 1.839m; LightGBM improves from 1.830m to 1.815m. Bias improves for both as well (XGBoost +0.409→+0.313, LightGBM +0.314→+0.241) - less over-prediction of clarity than v1's final config. Neither change is large, and CV RMSE is very slightly worse for both (v1's final tuning had already benefited from a feature-group stage that happened to pick unusually low-RMSE hyperparameters, which this stage's gap-aware objective trades away on purpose) - but the number that matters most, the one held-out look at test, moved in the right direction for exactly the two models where gap-aware-throughout tuning was supposed to help without a capacity ceiling getting in the way.

The neural net is worse in this version

The neural net's test RMSE moves from 1.839m (v1) to 1.888m, and its test bias grows more negative (–0.143→–0.297). Its mean train-val gap improved the most of any model (0.052→0.009) - gap-aware tuning did exactly what it was built to do - but a smaller gap on CV did not carry over to a better test number, echoing v1's own final-model finding that CV improvement doesn't always transfer to partition 5. At 3 layers of at most 8 nodes, the network has the least spare capacity of the three architectures to absorb a feature-set change (catchment_area_sqkm out, shoreline_flag in) without some reshuffling in what it learns to rely on; this is reported directly rather than smoothed over, consistent with how v1 reported its own final-model regression.

SDD-weighting on the actual final config

The production config above already applies SDD-weighting (k=2.0) to both tree models, carrying forward v1's finding without re-litigating it at the time. Re-checked here directly on this report's own final 16-feature+shoreline_flag config (not an earlier candidate): XGBoost top-quartile RMSE improves 2.610→2.485m and top-quartile bias moves toward zero (−1.41→−1.20m), but overall RMSE gets worse, not better (1.813→1.839m, exactly matching the production table above), and overall bias moves further from zero (+0.153→+0.313m) as the "rest" group (73% of the data) degrades on both metrics. LightGBM shows the identical shape. Weighting is retained anyway, matching v1's own reasoning: a deliberate trade for tail accuracy, not a claim that it wins on every metric.

Both changes requested at the end of v1 - gap-aware tuning applied systemically, and the shoreline-proximity flag - are now built, run end-to-end, and evaluated on the same untouched test partition v1 used. Net effect on the number that matters most:

Neither change was aimed at beating v1's single best-ever number in this project (XGBoost, optical+site+weather, pre-feature-selection, RMSE 1.747m) - that number was never reproduced by either version's own backward-elimination-and-final-tuning pipeline, v1's or v2's, because feature selection trades some of that richer configuration's raw accuracy for a smaller, more generalizable, cross-architecture-shared feature set. The relevant comparison is the two versions' own final, properly-gated production configs, and there v2 modestly improves on v1 for the two tree models while costing a bit of accuracy on the neural net.

QuestionAnswer
Does shoreline_flag earn a place in the model?Yes - survives backward elimination independently in all 3 models, the most consistent survival of any single feature addition tested in this project so far.
Does it change what the model is, fundamentally?No - optical attribution still dominates (61–68% depending on config); site's share grows modestly (to 25–36%) with one more real contributor in it.
Does systemic gap-aware tuning close the persistent partition-3 gap?No, and it was never expected to - partition 3's difficulty is a physical property of its basin (see the outlier-HUC investigation), not a tuning artifact. It does modestly shrink the average gap for 2 of 3 models.
Net effect on the final, deployable model?Small improvement for XGBoost and LightGBM (the two models SDD-weighting is already applied to in production); a small regression for the neural net.
